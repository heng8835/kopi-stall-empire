Your calls

Condensed milk is the sugar for Kopi and Teh. Kopi / Teh = condensed milk only; siew dai = less condensed, ga dai = more. The sugar can is for O and C drinks. That's how a real uncle makes it, and it means one less pour. "Kopi kosong" isn't on the menu (customers say "Kopi C kosong"). Approved
The pour bar sits near the top, but not too high. It docks just above the customers' order bubbles, not up at the clock, so your finger never covers it and your eyes don't travel far. The small meter beside the cup goes away. Approved

Recipes open up one by one

The recipe calendar

Each new order word arrives with a regular who wants it "their way", then a short tutorial card shows how to make it (steps plus the pour-bar zone). That day they order it more often, so you practise it. Shop machines add specials the same way: buy it at night, and next morning someone orders it, with a card.

    Day 1

    A quick first-day tutorial

    Uncle Tan's notes (about 2 minutes, skippable)

    1. Welcome card. Customers walk in and queue; the bubble is their order; the green bar is their patience.
    2. Make a Kopi together. Put a cup on the tray, hold the kopi pot over it until the bar says FULL, add condensed milk to NORMAL, wiggle the spoon, drag the cup to Aunty Lim.
    3. Make a Teh. Same steps with the teh pot. The cards get shorter each time.
    4. Make a Kopi O. "O" means no milk: sugar can instead, to NORMAL.
    5. Recipe book. The 4 recipes on one card, and where the book lives in the HUD (tap any time; the day pauses).
    6. Fame 名气. Perfect drinks raise fame, more fame brings more customers tomorrow, and walk-outs dip it a little. Nothing bad happens if you slip.
    7. The day. Clock and day bar: morning, lunch rush, teatime, closing. Then the stall opens for real and Episode 1 plays.

    Time of day

    A real wall clock, plus the day as a bar

    The hour hand sweeps from 7am to 7pm across the 90-second day; the minute hand spins so time visibly moves. Under it, the day as a bar with labelled periods. The marker crawls along it, and the clock rings a bell at last orders.

    MorningLunch rushTeatimeClosing
    7am open11am2pm5pm7pm

    Periods can mean something later (more office workers at lunch, kids after school, Ah Gong in the morning), but for now they're labels only.
